# Stage A2 — theorem transfer audit

**Status:** first targeted pass through primary papers and official sources. This is a transfer audit, not a claim that a literature search proves novelty or the main chess conjecture. The purpose is to identify which existing theorem supplies a real implication and which missing condition is genuinely about chess from `s₀`.

## Methods with no direct transfer

- **Graph cuts, dominators, and SCCs.** These operate on an already specified optimal-edge graph. They can certify that a target is beyond an unavoidable boundary, but a material-only quotient may have spurious paths because two projected edges need incompatible middle positions. An SCC condensation is exact for *state* reachability, not automatically for a projected material graph.
- **Strategy stealing and simple material dominance.** Neither a free move nor an extra piece is a universal advantage in chess: moves may change check, legal defenses, stalemate, or dead-position status. Any comparison theorem needs an explicit state simulation preserving legal replies and the WDL/DTM ordering; no such opening simulation was verified in this pass.
- **Sprague–Grundy and disjoint-sum combinatorial game theory.** Orthodox chess is a partisan, interacting board game with draws and alternating moves, not an impartial normal-play sum of independent subgames. The usual nimber decomposition lacks its hypotheses.
- **Proof-number search and proof games.** [Allis et al. (1994)](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/proof-number-search-2/) provide a proof-tree search method; [Elkies–Stanley](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/chess/spg.pdf) describe proof games as legal histories. Both are useful for certificates, but neither transfers a historical witness into an optimal opening witness.
- **Pawn/proof-game kernels.** No checked theorem in this pass simultaneously establishes a legal history and preserves every optimal opening prefix. Pawn-file accounting can exclude impossible histories but cannot by itself prove WDL or DTM optimality.

## A combined consequence and the missing lemma

The attractor theorem supplies exact WDL/DTM semantics; Cousot's soundness result and Clarke et al.'s simulation theorem then give a rigorous **conditional** recipe: build an abstract transition system containing every fully optimal transition from `s₀`, preserve the target material predicate, and prove no abstract path reaches the target. That would establish a real member of `H_v\R_v` after an independent historical and WDL-compatible witness. No cited theorem supplies the hard part: a compact, chess-specific proof that the abstract transition relation safely covers **all** optimal opening paths while excluding enough spurious ones.

The first missing lemma worth attempting is an *optimal first-entry frontier*: for one carefully chosen material family, prove an outcome-independent, finite cover of every first entry from `s₀` into a low material layer, retaining turn and irreversible-resource information. A separate exact local certificate would then rule out continuation to the target from every covered entry. This direction is preferable to enlarging local tables without an unavoidable-entry argument.
